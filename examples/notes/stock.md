# Slow broth

A good broth is mostly patience: bones, water and a heat so low that the surface only trembles. The note on [[simmering]] shows how brothy reads one.

## What you need

- [x] 1.5 kg of chicken bones
- [x] two onions, a carrot and a celery stalk
- [ ] cold water to cover everything

## How long to simmer

Bring the pot to a boil once, then turn the heat down until the surface barely moves. Simmer chicken broth for **3 to 4 hours** and beef bones for 8 to 12. Fish broth is done in 45 minutes: any longer and it turns bitter.

| Broth | Simmer | Skim |
|-------|-------:|------|
| fish | 45 min | once |
| chicken | 3–4 h | hourly |
| beef | 8–12 h | hourly |

## Skim as you go

A grey foam rises during the first hour. Lift it off with a flat spoon and leave the fat for later; it keeps the flavor and sets on top once the broth is cold.

## Salt late

Salt at the end. Reduction concentrates it, and a broth that tasted right at hour two is too salty by hour four.

> [!tip] Cold start
> Start the bones in cold water so the proteins release slowly and the broth stays clear.
